Two designs, two routines

The useful question is how often you need to change the screen’s height. The STAND-V001 uses a vertical pole as its height reference. The LX combines a lift mechanism with an articulated arm. Those layouts suggest different routines: setting a height and largely keeping it there, or changing positions more often.

That is an interpretation of the designs, not a report of how either arm feels in use. We have not tested installation time, wobble, desk damage, long-term durability or adjustment effort. We do not award a winner or attach scores to unmeasured performance.

A practical starting point: investigate the VIVO for a mostly fixed screen height after checking its limits. Investigate the LX when repeated height adjustment is a priority, provided your loaded display meets its minimum as well as its maximum. Neither shortlist establishes a safe fit for your desk.

The numbers need their footnotes

On the LX, a permitted load is not a promise of the full travel range. The dimensional drawing says that, above 20 lb, the lower portion of vertical movement can be reduced by up to 4.5 in. It also notes that a monitor deeper than 2.5 in may reduce capacity. Ask the manufacturer about a deep or unusually balanced screen rather than treating the load limit in isolation.

The LX clamp has positions, not an infinitely adjustable specification. The cited manual shows separate thickness configurations. Our checker retains a conservative nominal 0.4–2.4 in range, and flags values in the gaps between illustrated positions. It also sends thinner surfaces for review, even though the manual depicts a special thin-surface configuration. Confirm the actual assembly and metric measurements before choosing.

For the VIVO, an unspecified minimum is an unknown. The maximum desk thickness does not tell us that every thinner surface will clamp correctly. The checker explicitly leaves that minimum unresolved. Pole height and extension also describe different dimensions; neither is interchangeable with usable screen travel.

Make the choice at your desk

If the screen usually stays put

A pole-based arrangement is a reasonable layout to investigate when your routine is stable. Mark your desired screen position first, then compare it with the arm drawing. Do not assume the top of the pole is the height of your screen’s center.

If you change position often

The LX’s documented lift range is relevant to that routine. First establish that the monitor’s load falls within 7–25 lb, then review depth and movement qualifications. Our documents cannot tell you how the adjustment will feel or how stable it will be on your particular desk.

If the desk is the uncertain part

Stop the comparison and resolve the desk. A thick-looking honeycomb top, a glass surface, or a narrow beveled edge can raise questions the arm’s weight capacity does not answer. Get explicit confirmation from the desk and mount manufacturers for the intended mounting method.
