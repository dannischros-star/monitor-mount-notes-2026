Monitor arm FAQ: 18 questions before you buy or install

These answers are short on purpose. Follow the linked guides when the short answer exposes a configuration question.

Published October 27, 2026Document-based editorial · No hands-on testing

Compatibility questions

  • Does screen size determine compatibility? No. Check loaded weight, VESA pattern, depth, hardware, desk attachment and clearance. Read 27 vs 32 inches.
  • What do 75 × 75 and 100 × 100 mean? They are hole-center spacing in millimeters. They do not describe screw length or recess depth. See the VESA guide.
  • Can I use an adapter for an unknown VESA pattern? Only when the monitor, adapter and arm manufacturers support the exact combination. The checker does not approve adapters.
  • Is a 6 lb monitor always easy for an arm? No. The Ergotron LX 45-241-224 has a documented 7 lb minimum. Check both ends of a load range.
  • Can a 32-inch monitor fit a 22 lb arm? Possibly, but diagonal size is not enough. Compare actual weight, depth, VESA and the desk. Read the large-screen guide.

Desk and installation questions

  • Is a thicker desk always stronger? No. Thickness is one measurement; material, construction, damage and contact geometry also matter.
  • Clamp or grommet? Choose the attachment route that the desk and exact arm manuals support. See clamp vs grommet.
  • Can I clamp onto a beveled edge? Do not assume so. Measure the actual flat top and bottom contact and inspect the underside.
  • Can an existing cable hole be used as a grommet? Only if its size, location, material and hardware are documented as suitable for the mount.
  • Should I drill a new hole? Treat drilling as a structural modification that needs desk and mount documentation. It is not an automatic safety improvement.
  • Can I use any screw that fits the thread? No. Length, pitch, head and recess matter. Use the specified hardware.
  • Is there one universal torque setting? No. Follow the exact manufacturer procedure; this site does not invent a number.

Use and troubleshooting questions

  • How high should the monitor be? OSHA provides a baseline of top at or below eye level, direct-ahead placement and at least 20 inches viewing distance, adapted to the user and visual needs.
  • Why does my arm droop? Check complete load, minimum and maximum range, VESA hardware, plate seating, tension procedure and desk attachment. Stop if the desk or mount is damaged. See drooping troubleshooting.
  • Can I use an arm on a standing desk? Yes only after checking both seated and standing positions, cable slack, collisions and the desk’s moving load. See the standing-desk guide.
  • Do two monitors double the problem? They create two loads, two VESA checks and a new movement and desk-contact layout. Check per-monitor and combined limits.
  • Does an arm’s maximum extension mean I should use it? No. It is a boundary. Draw the desired position and the entire arm path.
  • Does an in-range checker result certify safety? No. This site’s checker is a manufacturer-spec preliminary filter. It cannot inspect your desk or installation.
  • What if the manual and this article differ? Follow the latest exact manual and ask the manufacturer. This site is an explanatory guide, not a substitute for instructions.

When a short answer leaves a detail unresolved, write down the exact model and the missing measurement. The goal of an FAQ is to identify the next document or question, not to turn an unknown configuration into a confident yes.

That discipline keeps the answer useful after the desk, screen or arm changes.
