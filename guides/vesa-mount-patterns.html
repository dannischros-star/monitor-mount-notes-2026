VESA mount patterns: measure the holes before the screen size

A 27-inch monitor can have a different mounting pattern from another 27-inch monitor. The holes decide the starting fit.

What VESA actually describes

VESA is a shorthand for a family of display-mounting dimensions. In this guide, 75 × 75 mm means the centers of the four mounting holes form a square 75 millimeters wide and 75 millimeters high. 100 × 100 mm describes the same idea at 100 millimeters. The measurement is center-to-center, not the outside width of the plastic recess and not the distance between screw heads.

The two arms in the current Monitor Mount Notes comparison list 75 × 75 and 100 × 100 mm patterns. That makes the pattern a useful first filter, but it does not establish that any monitor in the same size class will attach. Screen diagonal, panel type and resolution describe other properties. None of those properties substitutes for the hole pattern.

How to measure a monitor

Start with the manufacturer’s monitor specifications or manual. If the pattern is not listed, remove only the cover or stand hardware that the monitor instructions identify as removable. Never pry a decorative plate off because the holes appear to be behind it. With the monitor supported, measure horizontally between the centers of the left and right holes and vertically between the centers of the top and bottom holes. Use millimeters when possible; a small rounding error can turn a nominal match into a difficult assembly.

Look at the hole depth and the surface around it. A recessed plate may need spacers. A cable, vent or button may be close enough to interfere with a mounting plate. A hole can be threaded while still requiring a particular screw length. The mount manual and monitor manual must be read together because one document cannot know every other product’s recess depth.

Screws are part of compatibility

A matching pattern does not mean every screw in a drawer is acceptable. The thread diameter, pitch, length and head style need to suit the monitor and mount. A screw that is too long can damage internal parts; one that is too short may not engage enough thread. Spacers can change the effective length and the clearance around the plate. Use only the supplied hardware or the exact replacement specified by the relevant manufacturer.

Do not use resistance as a measurement tool. If a screw binds, stops early or needs unusual force, stop and check the thread and depth. Do not force a screw through a plastic recess or use a power driver to make a partial fit behave like a correct one. A clean, supported hand installation is a better compatibility signal than a screw that can be persuaded into place.

Recesses, adapters and unusual backs

Some monitors have a recessed mounting area, a removable stand interface or an offset pattern. A mount may provide spacers or a plate route for that design; another mount may not. An adapter plate can change the pattern, but it adds another interface, additional hardware and a new load path. The arm’s published capacity cannot automatically be transferred through an unapproved adapter.

If the pattern is 200 × 100, 200 × 200, an offset layout or unknown, mark it as unresolved in your worksheet. Identify the exact monitor model, the proposed adapter and the arm model when asking the manufacturer. “It looks close” is not an engineering specification. A reader should be able to explain which holes are used, which spacers are required and which document permits the arrangement.

Why the checker stays narrow

The Monitor Mount Notes desk-fit checker accepts 75, 100 or other/unknown. The first two can be compared with the two documented arm models. The other option deliberately produces an unverified result. The tool does not turn an unknown pattern into a recommendation, and it does not approve an adapter because adapters vary by model and hardware.

Record VESA pattern, screw details and recess depth as three separate lines on your worksheet. A pattern match is one gate, not the whole installation.

Pattern check worksheet

  1. Write the monitor’s exact model and the source where its VESA pattern is listed.
  2. Record horizontal and vertical hole spacing center-to-center in millimeters.
  3. Photograph or sketch the recess and nearby connectors for your own reference; do not publish private images in public issues.
  4. List the screw size, length and spacer requirements from the monitor and arm documents.
  5. If any item is unknown, pause before lifting the display onto the arm.
