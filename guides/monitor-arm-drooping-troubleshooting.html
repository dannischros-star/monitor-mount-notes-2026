Monitor arm drooping: diagnose the configuration before the tension

Drooping is a symptom, not a single fault. First make the setup safe, then isolate load, attachment, hardware and adjustment.

Published October 27, 2026Document-based editorial · No hands-on testing

Stop and support first

If the screen is falling, the clamp is shifting, a screw is backing out or the desktop is cracking, support the display and stop moving it. Keep hands out of pinch points while the arm is under load. A sagging monitor is not an invitation to turn every tension screw until the movement disappears; the wrong adjustment can conceal a hardware or attachment problem.

Do not continue if the VESA plate is separating, the monitor shell is damaged, the mount is bent, the desk surface is deforming or the arm cannot hold a stable position after the documented adjustment. Remove the load according to the manufacturer’s instructions and contact the arm or monitor manufacturer. A photo of the configuration and the exact model can make the question answerable without guessing.

Check the load range both ways

Read the exact model’s minimum and maximum load. The Ergotron LX 45-241-224 is documented for 7–25 lb. A display under 7 lb is not automatically easier for that mechanism, and a display over 25 lb is outside the stated range. The VIVO STAND-V001 lists a maximum of 22 lb; its cited product page does not establish a minimum monitor load. Do not add improvised weight to compensate for an under-range counterbalance.

Recalculate the load with the stand removed and all attached accessories included. A camera, light, adapter or laptop tray can move the effective load. For a large or deep display, check the manufacturer’s depth qualification as well as the number on the scale. Ergotron’s dimensions note that a deeper monitor may diminish capacity.

Check the VESA attachment

Confirm the plate is using the correct holes and that every screw is the specified size and length. A recessed back may need approved spacers. A screw that is too long can bottom out before the plate is tight; a screw that is too short may leave insufficient engagement. Look for a plate that is not seated flat, a spacer that has shifted or a hole that is damaged.

Do not tighten a screw against a plastic cover, connector or soft recess. If the plate twists when the display moves, remove the load and follow the monitor and mount manuals. The VESA pattern can be correct while the hardware installation is still wrong.

Check the arm and desk attachment

For an arm that sags at the elbow, inspect the documented tension adjustment and use only the manufacturer’s method. For a display that tilts at the head, inspect the tilt joint, plate seating and the screen’s center of mass. For a base that moves, inspect desk contact, clamp position and underside obstructions. A clamp can appear tight while one pad is resting on a rail or a rounded edge.

If the desk or arm has changed, repeat the fit assessment. A new monitor, an extension, a different clamp position or a reinforcement plate is a new configuration. Do not assume the previous tension setting or load result carries over.

A fault-isolation sequence

  1. Support the display and record what moves: elbow, tilt head, plate, base or desk.
  2. Compare the complete mounted load with the model’s minimum and maximum.
  3. Verify VESA holes, screw length, spacers and flat plate contact.
  4. Inspect the clamp or grommet route from above and below for movement or obstruction.
  5. Follow the exact tension procedure; stop when the manual does not cover the configuration.
  6. Recheck cable pull and collision loads after the arm holds position.

If the desktop is damaged or the mount is visibly bent, stop using the installation. Adjustment cannot repair a structural failure.
