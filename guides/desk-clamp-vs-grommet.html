Desk clamp vs grommet: choose the attachment path first

The arm is only as secure as the surface and attachment method that carry its load. Decide how it will attach before comparing reach.

Published October 27, 2026Document-based editorial · No hands-on testing

Two different ways to reach the desk

An edge clamp grips the front or rear edge of a desktop with upper and lower contact pads. It is attractive because it is reversible and usually does not require a hole. Its fit depends on a flat edge, the specified thickness range, an unobstructed underside and a surface that can carry the concentrated load. The clamp needs contact where the manual expects it, not merely a place where its screw can touch.

A grommet mount passes through a hole and spreads the attachment around a bolt or plate below the surface. It can be useful when the back edge is inaccessible or when the desk is designed with a suitable opening. It also introduces a drilling or hole-size question. An existing cable hole is not automatically a structural mounting hole; measure it and follow the mount’s instructions.

Edge clamp inspection

Measure thickness at the actual contact point. Rounded edges, bevels and thick lips can change the usable measurement. Look underneath for a drawer apron, metal frame, support rail, cable tray or a recessed panel. The lower pad must sit flat and remain clear while the screw is adjusted. If the clamp touches only a narrow corner or rocks on a radius, the number on the tape measure is not enough.

The VIVO STAND-V001 product page lists a maximum desk thickness of 3.25 inches but does not establish every minimum or material condition. The Ergotron LX materials state a nominal 0.4–2.4 inch span while the installation manual illustrates separate clamp positions. This is why a checker can flag an obvious maximum mismatch but cannot certify a clamp fit.

The attachment choice also affects how the desk is used afterward. Keep the base away from a drawer handle, cable pass-through or frequently handled edge, and leave enough room to inspect the hardware without dismantling the workspace. A reversible clamp is easier to revisit, but reversibility does not make an unsuitable contact surface acceptable.

Grommet inspection

For a grommet route, confirm hole diameter, hole location and the thickness that the bolt and plate can accommodate. Check whether the hole is centered where the arm base needs to sit and whether the underside plate can lie flat. A cable-management opening may be close to a cutout, edge or reinforcement that changes the load path. If the desk maker does not describe the hole as suitable for concentrated hardware, ask before drilling.

Drilling is a structural modification. Do not present it as a universal improvement. A hole through glass, a hollow-core panel, a stone-like top or a desk with hidden wiring requires the relevant manufacturer’s instructions and, where appropriate, professional assessment. The least invasive route is not always the strongest route, but an invasive route is never automatically safe.

A decision process

  1. Draw the base location from above and from the side.
  2. Measure contact thickness and inspect the underside with the desk empty.
  3. Read the exact arm manual for clamp and grommet routes.
  4. Ask the desk manufacturer about the material and mounting load if it is not explicit.
  5. Only then compare reach, height and everyday movement.

A reinforcement plate may spread load, but it also changes the geometry. Treat it as a new configuration that needs documentation, not as a universal safety fix.
