Monitor arms on standing desks: design the movement path

A standing desk turns a static monitor placement into a moving system. Test the path, not only the two endpoints.

Published October 27, 2026Document-based editorial · No hands-on testing

The desk moves first

A monitor arm attached to a sit-stand desk moves with the desktop. The screen’s relationship to the user can improve, but the entire base, arm, cable bundle and display also travel through space. A shelf, wall, lamp, microphone, laptop lid or neighboring monitor can block the route. The correct seated and standing heights are only the beginning.

Measure eye height in both postures and record the screen position that supports each task. The OSHA monitor baseline still helps: the display should be directly ahead and the top should be at or below eye level, adapted to the person and visual needs. The standing position may need a different screen angle or distance if the user changes how they lean or reach.

Cable slack is part of the installation

Route cables so the desk can rise and lower without pulling at the monitor connector, computer port or power brick. Measure the cable path at the highest and lowest desk positions, then add a deliberate service loop where the manufacturer or cable guidance permits it. Keep the loop away from a pinch point and away from the arm’s joints. A loop that looks generous at sitting height can become taut when the desk rises.

Check the cable chain with the display at the front and back of its intended arm range. A monitor arm can rotate while the desk moves, so two dimensions of motion may occur together. Use clips or channels that do not create a new snag or place pressure on a delicate connector. The right amount of slack is the amount needed for the intended path, not the largest loop that can be hidden.

Collisions and the wall line

Draw a side view of the desk, wall and display. Mark the screen at the highest useful standing position and the lowest useful seated position. Then draw the arm elbow in its folded and extended locations. Look for collisions with a shelf, wall, window trim or a desk-mounted accessory. If the desk is close to a wall, the arm may need a forward route that leaves less usable depth than its brochure reach suggests.

A wide or deep monitor increases the collision envelope. Above 20 lb, the Ergotron LX dimensional material says the lower lift range can be reduced; a heavy display may therefore need a different endpoint than a light display. The arm’s maximum movement is never a reason to use every inch of it.

Desk load changes while moving

The desk manufacturer’s lifting capacity and the arm manufacturer’s mount capacity answer different questions. The desk must carry the combined desktop load and tolerate the attachment method; the arm must support the screen and remain within its specified range. A reinforcement plate can change contact geometry, and a heavy display at full extension changes the way the desk feels. Obtain documentation for the actual desk and arm configuration.

If the desk top flexes, the clamp shifts, or the display changes position unexpectedly while the desk moves, stop and investigate. Do not solve an unstable setup by increasing tension or tightening a clamp without following the manuals. A moving desk adds opportunities for a small installation mistake to become a repeated load cycle.

A standing-desk rehearsal

  1. Set the seated position and mark the screen center, top edge and cable path.
  2. Set the standing position and repeat the marks without changing the desk’s intended user posture.
  3. Move the desk slowly through the full travel while watching cables, joints and nearby objects.
  4. Repeat with the display angled and rotated as it will be used.
  5. Inspect the clamp and desktop after the first day and after any desk or monitor change.
